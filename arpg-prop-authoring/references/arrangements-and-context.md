# Arrangements and camera-visible context

## Build a place with a reason

Work place by place rather than evenly sprinkling a checklist over an entire map. Give a small site a concise author-facing account: who used it, what they did, what changed and which few visible traces explain that. No player-facing text, new quest, wildlife simulation or interaction follows from this authoring note.

For a cold camp, benches face the hearth, bedding fits inside shelter, supplies sit near storage and a working person can reach the pot/wood. An axe/stump, cut firewood, boot-worn mud and forgotten sacks can imply departure. These are examples, not mandatory props in every camp. Fishing equipment faces reachable water; buckets/baskets belong within practical reach of the seated position. Fences have meaningful runs/openings, not isolated display segments.

Compose related objects as activity groups with circulation and breathing room. Quiet connectors still show restrained use through worn grass or a shortcut; not every screen needs a landmark or dense story cluster. Judge the relationships in an actual camera view, not just a valid ingredient list, non-overlap test or named camera.

## Structure an open environment

Use distinguishable local areas, paths, edges and landmarks as adaptable guidance. A pond, outcrop or believable livestock fence can split/rejoin routes and funnel movement through selected places without turning all Plains into corridors. The front of a small settlement can face a well/road; rear fences can explain why a route passes the inhabited side. Exact layouts, boss locations and gameplay gates need approved level scope.

Use foreground/midground/context and contrasts in density, height, surface wear and light to organize attention. Keep the player, enemy cues, loot, interactions and usable combat spaces readable. More assets or placements are not inherently better; build a representative convincing area before scaling it across a large level.

## Ground contact and boundaries

Ground transitions should be irregular and layered where the material calls for it: grass breaks up road edges, soil/wear relates to traffic and vegetation follows moisture/use. Avoid pasted rectangular surfaces and floating or uniformly planted objects.

The technical enclosing rectangle is not the authored visible level outline. Design irregular playable/scenic footprints, believable landforms and a camera-visible margin. Noise added to a rectangular slope does not by itself remove a square composition. Do not use a pile of props merely to conceal missing terrain, open seams or holes. Playable ground remains flat under the accepted current movement contract; shaped surrounding terrain does not authorize slopes/stairs for the character.

Because the camera looks beyond reachable areas, dress visible exterior margins coherently: the Fort entrance/rear yard and outdoor routes need surroundings beyond the blocker. Enclosed side-room cutaways may use intentional dark backing. Interior trees/roofs must not obscure the player; implement visibility/cutaway changes only through the approved native scope, preserving the relevant shadow rules.

## Reuse without obvious stamping

Reuse ingredients and related clumps, then vary relationships, rotation, height and edges where plausible. Preserve coherent geology and plant populations. Dense cattail/lupine patches are local placement choices, not an instruction to increase every canonical clump mesh or all scene limits. Check repeated groups at camera scale for recognizable stamps and excessive clutter.

Objects players reasonably expect to break in combat areas, such as barrels/urns/chests, must use the appropriate functional system or be omitted from that role. Town dressing is an accepted exception. This does not make every plank, pot or tiny flower interactive.

Keep editable arrangements distinct from runtime actors. Combined meshes, instanced ingredients, separate colliders and active objects depend on the existing implementation and approved behaviour; do not attach one body/collider/ordinary Actor to every decorative fragment by default.
